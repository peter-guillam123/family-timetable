Two children, two year groups, a timetable that runs on a fortnightly rota, and a school day divided by bells at eight different times. Somebody in this house is always asking which week it is. This page answers that.

What it does

It knows both timetables and it knows what time it is, so it can tell you where Tess and Finn are right now, what room they are in, who is teaching them and how long is left. It works out Week Red and Week Blue from the calendar rather than asking you. In the afternoon it switches to tomorrow's bag. In the morning it counts down to the first bell. If Food Technology is coming on Friday it starts saying so on the Wednesday, which is when the shopping has to happen rather than when the panic does.

It is also for Tess and Finn, not only for us. Their tab opens on their own day as a vertical timeline, which is the shape you want when the actual question is "what have I got after break". If you tell it who you are, it opens on the right tab next time.

The fortnightly rota

The week colour is worked out from one anchor date in the code, so it should be right without anyone touching it. The catch is that the rota pauses over half-term, so it drifts by one week after most holidays. When it does: press and hold the week badge in the top right and it shifts by one, and remembers. Uploading a new term's timetable resets it properly.

How it is built

It is four files of plain JavaScript, one stylesheet and no framework. Nothing to install, nothing to build, no dependencies to keep an eye on. Each term's timetable is one file that gets replaced. It caches itself so it still works on the walk to school where the signal goes, and you can add it to a phone home screen and it behaves like an app.

A note on what is public

The school name is not in here anywhere, and the page asks search engines not to index it. Teachers' names are, because "who do I email about the missing homework" is one of the questions this is for. That is a deliberate choice rather than an oversight, and it is one line to change if it stops feeling right.

Diary

10 September 2026 · milestone

Rebuilt from scratch

Gemini produced a first version of this and it was a decent skeleton: both timetables were in there, the bell times were right, and the idea of a live "where are they" card was the right idea. Three things were wrong with it, and one of them mattered a lot.

The small one: it re-rendered the entire page once a second to move a clock, which on a phone is a way of turning a timetable into a hand warmer. The medium one: on a phone it was a wide table you had to scroll sideways, which is the layout you get when nobody has stood in a kitchen at ten past eight trying to read it.

The one that mattered: Week Red and Week Blue were the wrong way round for Tess. Her real Week Red Monday is English, History, Chemistry, Computer Science and Maths, and that had been filed under Blue. Which meant Food Technology, the only lesson on either timetable that needs a supermarket trip, was being flagged in the wrong week. There was also no anchor date at all, so the week colour was just whatever the toggle happened to be sitting on when you opened it.

The fix for the fix is the interesting bit. Getting the label right today would have been a one-liner and wrong again by Christmas. Instead the colour now comes from a single anchor date, and the timetables were re-transcribed from the paper sheets rather than trusted. Finn's turned out to be correct all along, which is a useful reminder that a model being wrong once tells you nothing about whether it was wrong twice.

Also new: a morning mode that takes over before the first bell, kit warnings that come from a rule per subject rather than one hard-coded reminder about Food Tech, tickable bag lists, dark mode, and a teacher directory so the name at the bottom of an email means something.

10 September 2026

Rooms we do not know

Both sheets print UNKNOWN in the room column for every PE lesson, which is presumably the school's way of saying it depends on the weather. The earlier version filled those in with "Gym/Field", which reads like information and is not. They now say TBC, which is honest and slightly less useful, and that is the right trade.
